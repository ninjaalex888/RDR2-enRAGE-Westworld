# Changelog

## Unreleased (overnight 2026-10-27)

- Pacing: one place now decides whether something may happen, so the world stays ordinary most of the time and an encounter still surprises after hours of play. Each kind of moment has its own rarity and rests (since the last of its kind, at that place, for those people), and the big ones (the encounters) share a budget of two a night with a quiet stretch of about twenty real minutes after each. The crying woman, executions and gatherings keep their own chances and hours, and now also wait out that quiet. A test from the Testing page skips it and says so. F7: `TEST pacing:`.
- enRAGE's own people (its nemeses, gang men and named folk) are fully in. Their thoughts weigh what RealNPCs saw them live through with enRAGE's own record of them (what they feel about you and its memories of you, what they've heard, their goals, their mood), so what they think agrees with enRAGE (`[Mind] RecordMinds`). One of them may be the stalker while enRAGE has no use for them, one who hates you likeliest, handed back the moment enRAGE has, after 20 minutes at most (`[Stalkers] StalkerRecords`). When one of them dies of despair or after a street fight RealNPCs started, enRAGE's record of them says so in plain words (and marks the death if enRAGE missed it), and despair can reach them again (`[Mind] RecordFates`). Their clothes are never changed: enRAGE knows its people by their look. Settings version 68.
- What happened, and who saw it: RealNPCs keeps one plain record of every street scene, every deed of yours, every death it knows of, and every crying woman, execution and gathering: who did what to whom, where, when, how it came out, and who witnessed it (in clear sight within 30 m, or within earshot: 60 m for a shot, 25 m for a shout or a fight). Up to 400 are kept, and loading an older save drops the ones after it. The log gives each one (`EVENT ...`, with the debug log on), F7 the last five (`TEST events:`), and the log at load gives the save's size and what is in it (`SAVE ...`). It is what people's memories will be built from next.
- The Testing page has a row for every street scene now: an argument, a fistfight, small talk, gossip, a flirt, a courtship (the answer turns yes, maybe, no each time), a proposal, a drunk calling a man out, a purse lifted, a pickpocket caught, begging, a friend tending the hurt, someone keeping clear of the one who wronged them, a standoff, a song, a tall tale, haggling, a card cheat, two who are waking, and each of the exchanges (a snap at the worst of a worry, good and bad news, an old quarrel, sweethearts, a debt called in, a feud, a courting worry). The nearest named pair that fits plays it now, built exactly as a reason's scene would be; a test notes no tie or memory. Also: a plea at gunpoint (fire it, then aim at someone), a word as you ride into town, your waiting letters handed over (or a test one), a stranger's strange remark, a glitch, and disbelief over someone's head. **Show minds** (`[Testing] ShowMinds`, or its row) puts a small card over the named people within 15 m: who they are and their temper, their mood and worry, their ties and memories, and what is on their mind now. **Replay** fires the last test again with the same seed. Settings version 67.
- The Testing page lists everything RealNPCs can make happen on demand, in groups (encounters, people, street scenes, tools), one row each: Enter fires it (the panel closes, and it fires a second later), Right sets a key for it, Delete leaves it without one. Its keys are in a new `[Testing]` section (`TestKeys` switches them all off; settings version 66): the stalker (`]`), waking someone (`Y`), the execution (`[`) and the crying woman, hermit and gathering keys moved there with their values kept; the talk test (Shift+F7), the rough test (Shift+F9) and the font test (Shift+F2), which were fixed in the code, are settings there now, with those as their defaults. The panel refuses a key RealNPCs or enRAGE already uses, and says whose it is. Every fire logs `TEST fire <id>: started (...; skipped: ...)` or `refused: <why>`; a test skips the hour, place, chance and once-a-day rules, never the safety ones (no story missions, nobody while you have no control). F7: `TEST testing:`.
- A frame-time meter (`[Output] PerfMeter`, on; settings version 65). Every part of RealNPCs is timed each frame, its own time only. F7 writes the ten costliest and the whole add-on's cost (`TEST perf:`). The log says when RealNPCs averages over 1 ms a frame for 10 seconds, or one frame takes over 4 ms (`PERF: over budget`, `PERF: peak`), at most once a minute each, naming what cost most.

## 0.6.3 (not released yet)

All of 0.6.3 so far was built on 2026-10-26. Seen working in game: the execution on the road (both saving him and the shooting), the settings panel's Testing page, and women answering your flirting by chance (better, still being tested). Not yet seen in game: Minds, the despair act, news taking time, greeting first, the "Hook up" prompt, the CasualHookups fallback and the body-watch witness.

- Despair with a gun or a knife has an act now (Lex saw two people simply drop). They say their last words looking down at their hands. With a gun they draw it the way anyone draws, aim it at the ground in front of them for a moment, and the shot comes from the gun hand. With a knife the blade set is asked for early, and after the fall they writhe on the ground for a few seconds before going still. The game has no animation of a gun to one's own head, so this is pieced together from what it has. Despair is again never for enRAGE's own records (nemeses, gang men): harm to them waits for a way that tells enRAGE.
- People think about what is on their minds (`[Mind] Minds`, on; settings version 64; Lex's brief, roadmap 4 and 5, first slice). Before, a thought came from a dice roll over kinds of thought, and about half had no cause at all. Now each person keeps memories of what they lived through, and the thought that comes is whatever pulls hardest: a face put into the bar they saw an hour ago, the brawl they were in yesterday, the worry they're living through, the rain, someone in sight, you. Memories fade with time and with being thought about, come back when someone in them walks by, and weigh differently on each temperament: a coward and a hothead who saw the same fight never think the same line about it, and the man whose face went into the bar thinks of it differently from the one who did it. Nobody thinks the same line twice in a day. Strangers who saw something are known by how they looked ("a farmhand"), neighbours by name; up to three passers-by who saw a fight are kept so they can remember it. So far: scenes between people (fights, quarrels, slights, thefts, drunks, kindness, courting, talk), what they saw you do (a killing, a robbery, looting, stopping a thief, breaking up a fight, mercy at gunpoint: the timid never forget it, the cold shrug), deaths (a friend's grief for weeks, once word reaches them an hour or a few on; a foe's uneasy relief; a death seen with their own eyes), your flirting (a yes stays sweet, a no stays sore, a no from fear of you is its own thing, and it comes back to her when you walk by), and being saved from an execution. enRAGE's own people keep memories too, and one that weighs on them is what they think on the ticker. Anyone who saw it counts as a witness, the fleeing and lawmen included. Not yet: the weather turning. With the debug log on, every thought is logged with what it came from (`THOUGHT name: "line" <- source (pull)`); F7 writes `TEST mind:`. `Minds = false` gives the old mix.
- The settings panel no longer crashes on an action row (the memory wipe since it was added, and the Testing page's): the help line read an ini entry action rows don't have, and the panel switched itself off until a reload.
- A Testing page in the settings panel (Ctrl+F7 or Alt+F; Lex): keys to make each encounter happen near you now, out of sight, at any hour: the stalker (`]`), an execution (`[`), and the crying woman, a hermit's camp and a gathering of the awake (`CryingWomanKey`, `HermitKey`, `GatheringKey`, no key as they come; set one with Enter, Delete leaves it without). The same five things, and the memory wipe, are rows you can run from the page with Enter twice. A staged one doesn't use up that night's own; a hermit's camp still never goes in a town, since camps stay. Settings version 63.
- An execution on the road (`[Encounters]` `Executions`, on, `ExecutionChance` 20; settings version 61; Lex, after the game's own event with Zeke Townsend). Away from the towns, once a day at most, one of the nearest town's own regulars kneels at gunpoint, a mark on your map. Why he's there comes from his own life: the debt he's been worrying over (the creditor himself and a hired man, or men he sent, saying the same name and the same eleven dollars as his thoughts), the family he's feuding with, or talking to the law (the region's outlaws: the Lemoyne Raiders in Lemoyne, the Murfree Brood in Roanoke, the O'Driscolls elsewhere). He calls to you, they warn you off, and his thought shows over his head. Shoot or rush them and they fight you (no crime: they were holding a man at gunpoint). Save him and he's fonder of you for good, thinks of it for days, and word gets around of the stranger who saved him. Wait, or ride on, and they shoot him: his death is noted by their hand, his kin hear of it (as grief, never as a grudge against you), and the nearest town hears once he's found. If he vanishes before anyone sees him die, nothing comes of it. They give you 25 seconds once you've seen them (12 once they've warned you), with a last warning said to you a few seconds before; then he tries to get up and the gunman really fires (Lex's second test: the old way, a bullet from nowhere and health 0, showed no shot and looked like a loop). If you never come near, nothing comes of it. To see it now: press [ (`ExecutionKey`; settings version 62), or the settings panel's Testing page, "Stage an execution nearby". Seen working in game, the rescue and the shooting both. CONTENT NOTE: a man can be shot dead in front of you; `Executions = false` turns it off.
- News takes time, and needs someone to carry it (from a player's report: notices from strangers that "a stranger killed my friend", people angry within seconds about a camp of O'Driscolls killed out in the wilds, and a diary on one of them swearing revenge for a man killed 30 seconds before):
  - The corner's notices about people (their thoughts of you, their diaries, word reaching them, letters home, news of their lives) now come only from people you've met: talked to, seen a few times, or courted. Before, anyone with a memory of you counted. What strangers feel still happens; it just stays out of your corner. Several people grieving the same death make one notice a day, and a death's telegram home is one notice, not two.
  - Diary entries are written that evening, at least three game hours after what happened, and only if the writer is still alive.
  - Someone who knows what you did tells others only once they've lived another game hour, so a witness you kill straight after tells nobody.
  - A killing is talked of once someone who saw it has lived an hour to tell it. If everyone who saw it is dead too, or nobody saw it, nobody talks of it until the body would be found (6 to 18 game hours on), and then nobody knows who did it: the rumour says "somebody", the paper says "Nobody saw who did it", and nobody says it to your face.
  - Someone who comes upon the body within two minutes, while you're still within 30 m and in their sight, saw you by it: the killing is seen after all, and talked of an hour on if they live (Lex killed a man out of sight and someone walked up to look 5-10 seconds later).
  - A game hour is about two real minutes, so these are hours in the world, not long waits. enRAGE's own news of a killing reaches the dead man's friends 3 to 48 game hours on whether or not anyone saw; that part is enRAGE's.
- Greet her first (Lex): a woman you greeted (R) in the last three minutes is 15 points likelier to take to your flirting, and flirting cold (H without a greeting) is 15 points less likely. A yes to the flirting is no longer a yes to the night: that has its own chance, better after a greeting (the saloon's women 100 or 80 percent, single women 85 or 55, married ones 70 or 40). Once she says yes, Flirt in the lock-on menu reads "Hook up". If the night's answer is no, she says so when you ask, and a cold approach hears about its manners. CasualHookups' own "Flirt" on B keeps its name: that's its text, which RealNPCs can't change.
- Fixed: after a woman said yes and you asked her somewhere quieter, the screen could stay black with your controls off and the game's spinner turning in the corner. RealNPCs paused itself whenever the screen was dark, so it never faded back in, and its 12-second safety net was paused with it. The night now runs to its end in the dark.
- Flirting has more of the game's own voice: several recorded lines for each answer and each reason (a yes for your money, a no from a married woman, a frightened no, a maybe from the shy), tried in a different order for each woman and every few minutes, so the same woman doesn't always answer with the same recording. Arthur's own line tries the game's second-greeting compliments first. The log says which line played (`FLIRT voice:`), to learn which ones Arthur's voice and each woman's have.
- RealNPCs and CasualHookups (another mod) together (`[Romance]` `CasualHookupsDecides`, on; settings version 60). With CasualHookups.asi in the game folder, a woman's yes to your flirting leaves her willing for five minutes, and CasualHookups' own Flirt takes it from there instead of RealNPCs' fade to black. On a woman who hasn't said yes, its key is held. The first time, RealNPCs moves its Flirt from G, which enRAGE's own lock-on prompts (Interrogate, Give Her a Few Dollars) also use, to B, has her clothes come back after (enRAGE knows its people by their look), and sets it to always give in (0/100/0); a copy of the old file goes to `RealNPCs\backup` first. B is also the satchel key on foot; the first test will show whether pressing it while locked on opens the satchel too. If CasualHookups reads its settings only when the game starts, this counts from the next start. A night through CasualHookups counts as one here, and her thought after shows over her head. Not yet seen in game: whether holding the key stops its prompt (the log says, `HOOKUPS`).

It follows enRAGE's rule for the knocked out (out cold from fists, a gun butt or being incapacitated isn't dead), which RealNPCs didn't before: a resident knocked out in a brawl used to be counted dead.

## 0.6.2

- Trouble between people, played out in front of you, each time for a reason (street scenes; none of it seen in game yet, and each can be switched off on its own, `[Scenes]`, settings version 53):
  - Rough handling (`RoughHandling`): when two men who hate each other come to blows, and one of them is hot-tempered, proud or cold, he may grab the other and smash his face into the nearest table, bar, piano or wall instead, with the game's own move for it, picked to fit what's there. A hothead with drink in him now and then does it to a stranger in his way in a saloon. With nothing to hand it stays a fistfight. The victim swings back, walks off promising more, or runs, by his own nature. Those watching gasp.
  - Pickpockets among the townsfolk (`NpcThieves`): someone short of money (a debt, their savings, the bank, the drink) or light-fingered by nature (greedy, a drifter, dishonest) robs a well-dressed or drunk mark in passing, mostly unnoticed. Sometimes the mark finds it gone a moment later, and sometimes he catches the hand at once and the thief runs (catch him for the town's thanks). Never the loyal or the honest.
  - Drunks who call men out (`DrunkChallenges`): a drunk in a saloon at night picks a man, squares up with the game's own drunken moves, and falls on his face before a punch is thrown. He comes to a while later.
  - Begging: an honest person in the same money trouble asks a well-dressed stranger for a coin instead, and is given one or sent off, by the stranger's own nature.
  - Keeping clear: someone timid who was roughed up or robbed crosses the street to avoid the one who did it.
  - A small kindness: a friend of someone just roughed up or robbed goes to them, sees to the damage or offers a meal, and the two are closer for it.
  - Afterwards the two are tied by it, and know who did what: the one who did it and the one it was done to think about it differently, a lifted purse is a mystery to its owner, and the town talks about the face in the bar or the thief caught at it. Those who keep diaries write the day's scene down, from their side of it.
- People react to what's done and said to them (`[Scenes]` `Reactions`, on by default; settings version 53; not seen in game yet). In a street scene, a moment into each line, the one it's said to looks at the speaker and takes it by their own nature: a hothead or a proud man bristles at a threat or a jeer, a coward flinches, the cold shake their heads, the kind look down when they're mocked, a greeting or a kind word gets a nod, a drunk's ramble a puzzled look, and the everyday now and then a shrug. A man whose face has just gone into the bar reels as he cries out (the game's own street-fight hurt move where the game has it, else a wince). Someone leaning on the bar or sitting only turns their head, and nobody reacts when it's their own turn to speak.
- What's going on around them is on people's minds. A scene that just played out in front of them, the other people in sight, the weather if they're out in it, and the place they're in now come up in their thoughts. Who they're looking at is seen through their own nature: the poor envy the well-dressed, the kind pity the drunk, the timid keep an eye on a hothead, the proud sneer, the greedy size people up. Anyone known lately for putting a man's face into the bar, or for being caught with a hand in a coat, is thought of as that. And now and then a small pleasure or an old memory: the smell of bread, the first coffee, a tune stuck in the head.
- Self-defence lasts as long as the fight. It used to run out a minute and a half after your last punch or shot, even if the man who started it was still in the fight, drawing a gun on you or held at your gunpoint; whatever the game charged after that stood. Now it lasts until he's out of it, and a minute and a half after that.
- The ticker is clearer. It now shows only the thoughts of people you can see (on screen, in plain view), one at a time with a short pause between them (`TickerGapSeconds`, 3), and a small gold mark appears over the person whose thought it is (`ThoughtMarker`). With nobody in view thinking, the bar goes away instead of filling up with old news and a world summary. The ticker key on a thought moves to the next thought.
- The news has a box of its own: "Around town", in the top right under the "Sore at you" panel. Rumours, life updates, gang and faction news, diaries and what people elsewhere think of you show there one at a time, each for a few seconds (longer for a longer one, and paused while a menu is open), then the box goes away. `NewsTo` and `WarNewsTo` take a new choice, `corner`, now the default; an ini still on the old defaults (`ticker`, `right`) is moved over and the log says so. Settings version 38. Seen in game.
- No more white text. RealNPCs drew its subtitles and notices on the game's objective line, the white text at the bottom of the screen, and it showed words the voice wasn't saying (or no voice at all). Now it draws none:
  - Subtitles look like the game's own now: white serif text in a dark box, centred just above the ticker, where the game shows its own (Lex: "move our mod dialogue to this existing subtitle"). The game gives mods no way to put text into its own subtitles, so RealNPCs draws them the same way, and the plain white objective line isn't used for them any more. A test key, Shift+F2, shows the line in twelve of the game's fonts for 20 seconds, numbered, to find the face the game's subtitles use (`FONT test` in the log); line 1, `$body`, was closest, and the subtitles use it.
  - Subtitles only for what's said to you, and only while the voice is playing. What people say to Arthur shows on the game's subtitle line while their recorded voice plays: the recording's own words when the word list knows them, else what they mean. A line whose voice doesn't play gets none, and talk you only overhear (street scenes, conversations between others, mutters) never does. The game's own Subtitles option has to be on too. `[Output]` `Subtitles` is `on` (the default; `matched` becomes it) or `off`; settings version 47. Each one is logged (`SUBTITLE Name: "..." (the recorded words | the written line)`). The game gives mods no way into its own dialogue subtitles: a test played one greeting eight ways (to you, to another person, to nobody, with each of the speech call's flags, and the game's scripted-speech way) and the game subtitled none of them, so its subtitle line it is.
    - Notices (the start-up line, F7's status, hints with the ticker off, a test key's answer, news sent to the game feed) are the game's own updates at the top left, or the "Around town" box if the game won't take them.
