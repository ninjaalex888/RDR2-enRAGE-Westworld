# Changelog

## 0.6.0

- New name: enRAGE Westworld is now **RealNPCs** (`RealNPCs.dll`, data folder `Documents\Rockstar Games\Red Dead Redemption 2\RealNPCs`). On first start the old `enRAGE-Westworld` folder is copied over (saves, diaries, voice files; the ini is renamed `RealNPCs.ini`), and the old folder is left as it was. If `EnrageWestworld.dll` is still in the scripts folder, RealNPCs stays off and says so, so two copies never run together.
- Two master switches. `WestWorld` (default on) turns the whole West World side on or off: the Awakening, hosts wondering if the world is real, glitches, reveries and revenants. Off, nothing of it happens and people already waking go quiet, without losing their place; pending reveries wait instead of being cancelled. `Stalkers` is its own master with its own section: chance, day and night hours (`StalkerDayFrom`/`To`, `StalkerNightFrom`/`To`; the 05:00 stalker day is unchanged), how far behind they appear, follow and stare distances, how long you must stand still, what they do then (`StalkerWhenStill`: circle, watch, or rush, which stops a few steps short and never attacks), circle distance, how far they run off, and whether they mutter. Odd values are put back in order. A stalker who is someone you killed is a West World thing and needs both switches.
- A new settings panel. Alt+F11 now opens the RealNPCs settings panel instead of enRAGE's own (it closes enRAGE's through enRAGE's own toggle; `ReplaceEnrageSettings = false` gives Alt+F11 back to enRAGE). Text about half as large again, and everything in one place: West World, Stalkers and the rest of RealNPCs first, then every enRAGE section. Settings under a switched-off master are greyed out. Choices cycle (StalkerWhenStill, NewsTo, WarNewsTo), keys are set by pressing them, and every change takes effect at once: RealNPCs reloads its settings and shows what it really runs with if a value was out of range; enRAGE is asked to reload NemesisMod.ini the way its own panel does, and the panel shows what enRAGE kept. Only the one value is changed in either file (spacing, comments, line endings and encoding stay as they were; tested byte for byte), and NemesisMod.ini is backed up to `RealNPCs\backup` once per session before the first change. enRAGE's own bookkeeping entries stay hidden, as in its panel, and the keys enRAGE checks itself are handed to its own panel. `SettingsKey` (Ctrl+F7) opens the panel too. A pad works while it is in use (D-pad, LB/RB, A, B closes). Holding Left or Right shows the value at once and writes it once it settles. enRAGE's pages appear only when RealNPCs can tell which of its entries are its own bookkeeping and can ask it to reload; otherwise a page says why. Choosing a key that enRAGE also uses gets a warning.
- The panel has one Hotkeys page (every RealNPCs key, then enRAGE's own `[Keys]`; the keys are no longer on their old pages) and one enRAGE page with all of NemesisMod.ini, each section under a small heading, instead of a page per section. Shift+Up/Down (or X with the D-pad) jumps to the next or previous section; the help line says which enRAGE section a setting is in. "Keys and notices" is now "Notices and the panel".
- `AwakeningDespair` can now be set anywhere from 0 to 100 percent (it was capped at 10). Settings version 15.
- `AwakeningBreakdown` (90): how often an unravelling or awake person falls apart (kneels, shouts, runs) rather than holding it together.
- The ini is reorganised into `[WestWorld]`, `[Stalkers]` and `[Voices]` (settings version 13); values are kept. The game runs on a copy with the master switches applied; the ini always keeps the player's own values.
- The ticker is always one line high now, so it no longer overlaps the game's subtitles just above it. Text a little too long is set slightly smaller; text much too long is shown in parts, one after another, in the same bar.
- `TickerNextKey` (T) in `[Ticker]` shows the next line on the ticker now; on a line shown in parts, the next part first. From someone's thought it moves on to the news, so pressing it again near the same person doesn't bring the same thought back. T is also enRAGE's telegraph key, which only does something within a few steps of a telegraph clerk; there, both happen. Settings version 16.
- New defaults, from play-testing: `AwakeningDays` 1 (was 6), `AwakeningBreakdown` 90 (was 35), `AwakeningDespair` 75 (was 2), `ReturnChance` 35 (was 15), `StrangerReturnChance` 15 (was 5). An ini that still holds an old default is raised to the new one when it is updated, and the log lists which; values set by the player are kept. Settings version 17.
- Stalkers do odd things when you stand still. Two new `StalkerWhenStill` values: `eerie`, where he comes to about `StalkerActMetres` (default 12), faces you and works through the game's own emotes and scenarios one after another with a stare between (46 of them, among them digging a grave, scrubbing blood from the ground, kneeling at a graveside, lying down like the dead, writing in a notebook, pointing at his eyes and then at you, a finger to the lips, waving, beckoning, howling at night; some gestures are done twice exactly alike, stop dead partway, or go slow; and two that play on not being seen: he creeps closer, crouched if the game lets him, only while he is off your screen and stops dead when you look, or he is standing somewhere else round you each time you look away and back), now and then moving to another spot round you; and `mix`, the new default (eerie 60%, circle 20%, watch 10%, rush 10%). Move and he stops at once and runs off. Nothing in the list draws a weapon. Each act is checked as it plays: one that doesn't load, play or start is tried once more, then dropped for the session (for men or women separately), and the log says which (`STALKER act ...`). An ini still on the old default `circle` moves to `mix`. Settings version 18.
- Who stalks you, and when, is new. The daily roll and its day and night windows are gone (`StalkerDayOnly`, `StalkerNightOnly`, `StalkerBoth`, `StalkerDayFrom`/`To`, `StalkerNightFrom`/`To`). Instead, every `StalkerEveryMinutes` (34; it was 10 at first) real minutes of play there is a roll at `StalkerChance` (20 percent, now per roll; older inis start again at 20, and the log says so). On a hit, someone already near you (within `StalkerNearbyMetres`, 60, and at least 12 m off) stops, stares and starts following you: the one with the most history with you (`Core/StalkerPick.cs`: an enRAGE townsperson with a grudge or who saw what you did ranks highest, then residents who saw or heard of your deeds or talked with you, then any passer-by). enRAGE's own people are never taken over, since enRAGE runs them; nor lawmen, children, story characters, anyone indoors, mounted, fighting or in a scene. They are handed back to the game afterwards, never deleted. If nobody near can follow you for three minutes, a stranger turns up behind you instead (`StalkerStrangers`, default on). Someone you killed coming back as a stalker works as before. Settings version 19.
- `StalkerKey` (`]`) makes a stalker now: someone near you, or a stranger at once if nobody near can be. It replaces Shift+F7. Digit keys can now be used for any RealNPCs key, and in the panel Left/Right no longer changes a key setting as if it were a number.
- Keys moved: `WakeKey` G -> Y and `StalkerKey` 0 -> `]`. An ini still on G or 0 moves over (the log says so); a key of your own is kept. Symbol keys (`[ ] - , . / \ '` and the backtick) now work for any RealNPCs key, and the panel writes keys as you'd type them (`]`, `0`). Settings version 20.
- `ReaderKey` (letters, diaries and clippings) moved from F5 to `N`. An ini still on F5 (or on `-`, briefly the default) moves over (logged); a key of your own is kept. Settings version 26.
- The Windows voice is gone (`Game/Tts.cs` and the `[Voices]` settings `SpokenVoice`, `VoiceVolume`, `VoiceMan`, `VoiceWoman`, `VoiceVariety`; settings version 27). Every line that was read by it now plays the speaker's own recorded game line: stranger musings and glitches an unsettled one (a new `uneasy` set: WHATS_GOING_ON, GENERIC_SHOCKED_MED, NO_IDEA, GENERIC_SHOCKED_DISBELIEF) unless the line's tone finds a better fit, uncanny remarks and delayed lines the same, and the stalker's mutters his own voice from where he stands (a taunt when nothing fits). Where a recording's words are known (`voices\words.txt`), the subtitle shows them and the written line goes to the ticker as the thought.
- Quiet while you stand still (`[Quiet]`). After `QuietStillSeconds` (3) without moving, or whenever the cinematic camera is on, RealNPCs asks enRAGE for its own calm period (`Grace`, the one it uses after a load, a fast travel or a train), ten seconds at a time, renewed every seven while it lasts. enRAGE checks that calm before every random encounter, raid, war patrol, war encounter, road ambush and faction response, and its tension counter holds ("settling in"). So nothing new comes for you, and it carries on within about ten seconds of you moving. Fights already under way carry on; enRAGE's town regulars wait too. Needs enRAGE's `[Calm] Enabled` (default on; the log warns if it's off). `QuietWhenStill` and `QuietInCinematic` switch each part off. Settings version 21.
- Self-defence is no longer noted as a crime. enRAGE's fighters (its roles Active, Crew, HiredGun, Patrol, Skirmish, Hunter, Rival, Camp, read with its own `RoleOf`: the ones it marks red on the map) count as having come at you even if you fire first. While you fight off people who came at you, the game's murder, assault, unarmed and lasso assault, threatening and disturbance crimes are suppressed each frame (`SUPPRESS_CRIME_THIS_FRAME`), so no witness runs to report and no bounty comes of it: right after each hit on them, while one you hit is still alive, and while you aim at one; never once you've hurt anyone who hadn't come at you within five minutes, or another crime of yours was confirmed, and never in story missions or against lawmen. Who came at you is decided at your first hit and never changed: enRAGE saw them come at you (its own `_cameAtYou` and `_selfDefence`, read by name), or they were fighting you before you hurt them and you hadn't drawn on them in the last 10 s, or they were hostile to you (relationship hate: red), or it's a story mission. Killing them makes no deed (no `kill`, `murder` or `namedkill`), so no witness memories in enRAGE's records, no rumour, town talk, posse talk, newspaper or legend; enRAGE still learns its people died, and reveries still happen. A game-confirmed assault while everyone you hurt came at you isn't noted either. Lawmen and anyone who begged still count, and someone you hit first stays yours however long they fight back. The bounty and wanted level you had before such a fight are kept until 90 s after your last hit or kill on someone who came at you (`SelfDefenceNoBounty`, default on; the lowest bounty of the 4 s before the first hit, as reports lag): any rise is put back and a new wanted level cleared, with a `SELF-DEFENCE: bounty ...` log line. It stands aside if, in the 5 minutes before the fight or during it, you hurt anyone who hadn't come at you (a lawman included) or a crime of yours was confirmed (an assault while only defending excepted), and during story missions, which set their own law. A fight that moves more than 300 m or into another state starts again from the bounty there, so a bounty owed elsewhere is never wiped (the decision is `Core/DefenceLaw.cs`, tested offline). Crimes the game confirms that RealNPCs doesn't know are logged during such a fight. enRAGE's FairGame does the same for its outlaws; this covers soldiers and everyone else. Settings version 22.
- The same people at the counters (`TownPosts`, `Core/Posts.cs`). Shot down, by default (`TownPostsAfterDeath = survive`), a counter keeper survives, as the game shows by bringing the same model back bandaged: the same person, wounded (lines for the first three days and after), who knows you did it if anyone saw or you were close, and is afraid, bitter or coldly polite by temperament when you walk in. When they're back, the people who saw them fall grow more aware in West World, and the town hears. `successor` makes the death final, as below. 46 counter trades in 13 towns, by the game's own unique models (general store, gunsmith, butcher, doctor, barber, barkeep, hotel, undertaker, photographer, station, trader, stable, tailor), are each one person for good: named, saved, never dropped from a full save, remembering how often you come in (a new face, then a regular), with thoughts of their own trade on the ticker ("Name, the gunsmith"). When one dies, by any hand, the post passes on the next time the game staffs the counter: to a relative of the same sex as the model (son, brother, nephew, cousin, son-in-law; daughter, sister, niece, daughter-in-law), sharing the family name where they would, or to a newcomer who bought the business. Successors grieve (differently in the first three days), compare themselves with the one before, and know you did it if you killed them in front of anyone. If the model has other outfits, a successor is dressed in one, only while nobody can see; the log reports how many outfits each model has (`OUTFIT probe`). They stay at their counters: no scenes, stalking, walking wake acts or despair. Sheriffs, deputies, preachers, auctioneers and mission characters are left out. F7 lists every post. Settings version 23.
- Night encounters (`[Encounters]`, `Core/Encounters.cs`, `Game/WestworldScript.Encounters.cs`). Every `EncounterEveryMinutes` (12) real minutes of play there is a roll for each kind that's on, when the time and place fit, at most one running at a time and each at most once a night (noon to noon). Never in a mission, while wanted or fighting, indoors, in water, while a stalker, a street scene, a wedding, a plea or a theft is going on; stalkers wait while one runs. The first is **the crying woman** (`CryingWoman`, `CryingWomanChance` 15): between 22:00 and 04:00 in the wilds (no town), a woman is put out of sight 30-55 m away, kneeling as at a graveside, calling for help every 7-12 s in her own recorded voice (SCARED_HELP, PANIC_HELP, GENERIC_FRIGHTENED_HIGH) with a subtitle ("A woman, somewhere in the dark"). Within 6 m, or with a gun on her within 25 m: half the time she draws a knife and attacks for about 4 s, then runs, and once out of sight she kneels and cries again somewhere else (go to her a second time and she only talks and walks away); 35% two or three men with revolvers step out 14-26 m away and attack while she runs (she manages alone if the men can't be placed); 15% she says something that makes no sense and walks off into the dark. Attackers are put in their own relationship group, hostile both ways with yours, and counted as having come at you, as is the woman who brought the men, so it's self-defence (no deed; a bounty the fight brings is put back, and killing one keeps that going as hitting one does). The knife woman is taken out of the fight before she runs, so she really runs, and is never moved while you can see her. Every roll that does nothing says why in the log (a town, the hour, already out tonight); a woman who couldn't be placed doesn't use up the night. It ends if you ride 160 m away, after 8 minutes, when the attackers are down, in a mission or on your death; the living vanish only out of sight, the dead stay where they fell. Logged as `ENCOUNTER crying woman: ...`; F7 adds a `TEST encounters:` line (next roll, last reason a roll was skipped). Settings version 29.
- The hermits who woke (`Hermits`, `HermitChance` 25; needs `WestWorld`; `Core/Engine.Hermits.cs`, `Game/WestworldScript.Hermits.cs`). A resident who runs in the wake acts' escape ending, fully awake, is marked as fled (`Resident.Fled`) and is never recast on a passer-by again. On a roll in the wilds (once a day, at most 8 camps) the longest-fled without a camp, or a made one (stage 4, gone some days) when nobody has fled, gets a camp 50-80 m off and out of sight, saved with the resident (camps are never dropped from a full save). The first time, they shout once at the voice ("Someone, off in the trees"). Whenever you come within 120 m of a living hermit's camp and the spot is out of view, they are there with a campfire (`p_campfire01x`, else `p_campfire05x`; the log says if neither loads), sitting (`WORLD_CAMP_FIRE_SIT_GROUND`) and muttering at the voice when you're within 35 m, or asleep (`WORLD_CAMP_FIRE_LAY_GROUND_SIDE`) from midnight to five, waking only when you're within 10 m. Within 18 m they stand and keep you in front of them; within 10 m they warn you off (up to three times, in their own threatening voice); 8 s watched at a distance and they tell you how it was, three things a visit, each once until all eight are told (`PersonState.Told`). Closer than 7 m for 5 s after a warning, 4 m at any time, aimed at within 40 m or hurt, and they run, and aren't back until you've gone 180 m away and returned. Never armed. Their thoughts are on the ticker ("Name, living rough"), half their own, half the awake lines. Killing one counts as a killing (Watch.cs lets their death through). F7: camps, the nearest, how many are waiting for one.

## 0.5.5

- Stalkers can appear again. In 0.5.4 every spawn spot was refused ("no walkable ground behind you" on every try, even in Valentine), so no stalker ever showed up. The game's safe-spot answer is now read in both memory layouts, and if it still says no, the stalker stands on plain ground behind you, never inside a building. Staged weddings use the same fix.
- Stand still for 20 seconds and the stalker runs in close (6 to 10 m) and circles you. Take a step (on foot or on horseback) and he runs back out to 45 to 70 m, choosing a spot hidden from you, ideally just round a corner or behind a tree, and watches from there. Turning the camera doesn't count. Walking up to him while he circles no longer ends the stalking; drawing on him or hurting him still does.
- A stalker keeps it up for `StalkerMinutes` in full (it used to be cut short by up to a third). Default 120, ceiling 240 (was 8 and 60); an ini still on 8 or 60 is raised to 120. Settings version 8.
- More lines are voiced. A new tone reader (`Core/Tone.cs`) picks a recorded game line that fits each line's feeling (anger, threat, fear, grief, thanks, gossip, flirting, brush-off, disgust, greeting, goodbye), weighed by how the speaker feels about you. It now covers conversations with enRAGE's people and townsfolk (up to three voiced lines each, not the opening greeting the game already answers), passing remarks, replies to insults, flirting on both sides, couples and strangers chatting, and funeral mourners. The voice plays when its subtitle appears. The player's own lines use Arthur's or John's voice. Muttered lines stay silent and uncanny ones keep the Windows voice.
- The Awakening, first part. A share of the living (`AwakeningShare`, default 5%) is quietly chosen to wake, leaning toward those who already suspect the most, and moves through four stages (stirring, questioning, unravelling, awake), `AwakeningDays` game days apart. Their thoughts, remarks and diary days change with the stage; from the second stage they keep a secret diary entry now and then. They count toward the ticker summary ("three are waking up"). Settings version 9. The wake key (`WakeKey`, G by default) wakes the person you are facing by one stage so you can see it at once (the log says `AWAKENING <name>: stage N`). Odd behaviour on screen, spreading, and breaking the script come next.
- Everyone is someone. Ordinary people who are not enRAGE's (most of the world) now get an identity of their own the moment something asks who they are: a name, a trade, a town, a temperament. Press the wake key near anyone and they wake a stage, not only enRAGE's few named people; the ticker names the passer-by whose thoughts it shows, and a woken stranger thinks woken thoughts. These residents live only in this add-on: nothing about them is written to enRAGE. They are saved once something happens to them (up to 400, oldest untouched first); about 5% start already stirring when first met. The save format gains a resident list (older saves load unchanged).
- The Awakening on screen. People who have begun to wake now behave differently when you are near: stirring ones glance at you a moment too long or stop and stare; questioning ones stand perfectly still watching you or pace the same few steps back and forth; unravelling ones freeze mid-step and pace faster and shorter with pauses, as if stuck in a loop; awake ones walk straight up to you, face you and say something lucid. Only ordinary people are directed (residents and enRAGE's named townsfolk; enRAGE's full records stay with enRAGE), never during story missions, scenes or danger, and they are handed back as they were. A person who woke in a town may turn up again there in another body of the same build. The wake key's answer now shows at once instead of queueing behind other subtitles. The log lines are `WAKE act: <name> (<stage>) <act>`.
- Ordinary people's inner lives are written to a psychological brief: the body (a blister, hunger, a craving for tobacco), how they think they look to others, the fear of losing what little they have, the age pressing in (the railway, the Pinkertons, consumption, cholera, the bank), the gap between the face they show and what they feel, and, when you are close, granular things they notice about you (a gun hand, how you sit your horse). Short, fragmented, sensory.
- Every adult in the world can be a person with a mind, soldiers and lawmen included, not only enRAGE's named people: the wake key and the ticker now give soldiers, lawmen and script-owned people a name, a trade and thoughts of their own. Their movement is left to whoever owns it (enRAGE's people and script-owned soldiers only get the passive part: head turns, thoughts and a voice); ordinary people no script owns get the full acts.
- Much fuller acts at every stage. Stirring: double-takes, stopping to look round at nothing. Questioning: standing perfectly still, pacing the same steps, walking out to see where the town ends, asking a neighbour if they feel it too. Unravelling: freezing mid-step, erratic pacing, shouting at the sky, kneeling, running with no goal. Awake is now a lasting state: they come to you, say two or three things (who is writing this, is there an outside), then end in one of: pleading with you, trying to leave town, trying to wake someone, or refusing to get up. Pressing the wake key on someone now ends what they were doing and shows the new stage straight away.
- A rare breakdown, with a setting. An awake ordinary person who is armed, alone with you at least a few steps away, and not owned by a script, has a small chance (`AwakeningDespair`, default 2 percent, 0 turns it off) of not being able to bear it and ending their own life with their own gun. It happens to residents only, never to enRAGE's people or townsfolk, is theirs alone (not counted as anything the player did), and is rolled once for each awake person. Unarmed, they kneel instead. Settings version 12.
- Acts and words now belong together. Each woken person's act comes with thoughts from the same moment: pacers count their steps and the number of times they have walked it, people who freeze lose their sentence halfway, starers think about being looked back at, and the one walking up to you thinks about why. The thoughts show on the ticker as they happen.
- Ordinary people have inner lives of their own. A passer-by's thought now comes from their trade, their town (Valentine's cattle, Rhodes's feud, Saint Denis's trolleys and docks, the Annesburg mine...), their temperament, their faith or the hour of the day, and is not repeated soon, instead of one shared list of ten lines.
- Gang and faction news (wars, feuds, clashes, rivalries, unrest, rank changes, jobs) now shows as the game's pop-up on the right of the screen, apart from the bottom ticker. People's own life news and what they think and do stay on the ticker. New setting `WarNewsTo` (right or ticker). Settings version 11.
- Waking someone with the wake key now makes them act straight away so you can see what the stage does (a stare, pacing, or the walk to you), woken people act more often, and the log says how each act ends (`WAKE act ended: ...`).
- The log records the game's first three safe-spot answers (`SAFECOORD probe`), and the stalker's failure line counts how often plain ground was used.

## 0.5.4

- Daytime stalkers. Each in-game day rolls one of four outcomes: no stalker, one by day (06:00 to 17:00), one at night (18:00 to 04:00), or both. `StalkerChance` is the chance that someone follows you that day, and the new `StalkerDayOnly`, `StalkerNightOnly` and `StalkerBoth` settings (25 / 50 / 25) set how a stalker day splits.
- A daytime stalker who never gets a clear moment before dusk is dropped. A night stalker still waits until the next night.
- The log shows each day's roll, and F7's status line lists the stalkers waiting.

## 0.5.3

- Stalkers fixed. One due after midnight was wiped out when the day changed, and one that couldn't find a hidden spot was given up for the day. A stalker who is due now waits for the first clear moment at night (outdoors, no story mission, no lawmen after you, not fighting), and comes the next night if this one runs out.
- The log says when a stalker is due and what he's waiting for, and F7's log dump includes the stalker's state.
- Shift+F7 calls a stalker right away, for testing.
- Old saved talk that read "Pvt. saw ..." or "Pvt.'s brother" (from before 0.5.1) now reads "a private saw ..." and "a private's brother".

## 0.5.2

## 0.5

- More life in the street. Strangers now also square off in standoffs, sing old songs drunk, tell tall tales (some about you), read the paper aloud, haggle over horses, fall for a snake-oil pitch, make small talk and pick pockets. After a bad crime, men talk about getting up a posse. The widow of a man you shot may be grieving in town, and may recognize you.
- Step into a scene: greet or antagonize someone in it. Take a side in a quarrel, break up a fight or a standoff (the town hears you're a peacemaker), or egg it on.
- Pickpockets: when one strikes near you, run him down (lasso, hogtie, tackle) and the town hears of it. Shoot him and it's a killing.
- Hold a gun on a stranger and they beg, with a name, a family and a trade. Lower the gun and word gets around that you showed mercy. Shoot anyway and that's what they'll say about you.
- Riding into town: locals react to what they've heard of you, your bounty, or your good name.
- The game's own voices are used far more: most of the add-on's lines now come with a fitting recorded line in that person's real voice (cheering, mocking, pleading, gossip, thanks...).
- The Windows voice can be any voice you have installed, and each speaker keeps their own voice, pitch and pace.
- Westworld: more host lines and conversations, people returning to one day of their past, strange dreams in their diaries, and a few lines they find in their heads (Shakespeare, Poe, Dickinson).
- Everyday thoughts from passers-by on the ticker.

## 0.4

- The ticker: a line at the bottom of the screen that's always there. It shows what the people near you are thinking and, when nobody is near, a summary of the world and the latest news.
- Your deeds matter: towns and shops you visit, church, the saloon, hunting, killing, robbing, stealing horses, cheating at cards, sweet-talking, letting a beaten man go. People who saw it remember; people who didn't hear about it.
- Rumours travel from town to town, about one town a day, and get bigger and stranger the further they go.
- Your deeds turn up in enRAGE's newspapers.
- Letters wait for you at post offices: from the kin of people you killed, from people you were good to, from sweethearts, from people who suspect the truth, and wedding invitations.
- Diaries: loot a body (or search a gang camp) and you may find the diary they kept. Read it, and your letters and clippings, with F5.
- Kin far away hear when you kill someone. Some write. Some come west for you, as one of enRAGE's own people with a grudge.
- In someone's home town, a passer-by may turn out to be their mother or brother.
- Strangers around you flirt, quarrel, call in debts, catch cheats, propose and come to blows. In saloons they trade rumours, and loose talk can give away a gang job.
- Weddings happen at the church. Be there and the couple will remember it. Shoot it up and they'll remember that instead.
- Mourners at enRAGE's funerals speak of the dead.
- People go after what they want, owe each other money and collect it, and get moved out of jail.
- Westworld: some people start to question their world, out loud, read by the Windows voice. Now and then someone glitches.
- Reveries: some of the people you kill wake up again days later and remember how they died and who did it. Strangers you killed can come back too.
- Stalkers: rarely, someone follows you from a distance and hides when you look.
- Personalities colour what people say: a hothead, a coward, a proud man, a cold one.
- Changes to the ini take effect in game within a few seconds.
- Story missions are left alone: no scenes, stalkers or staged weddings while one is running, and nobody from a mission comes back as a revenant.

## 0.3 and earlier
