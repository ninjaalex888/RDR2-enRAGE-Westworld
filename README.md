# RealNPCs

> **Early access: a work in progress.** RealNPCs is actively being worked on. New features arrive often, some parts are still rough, and things can change between versions. Feedback and bug reports are welcome.
>
> https://buymeacoffee.com/lexthestoic

An unofficial add-on for **[enRAGE](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/10967)**, the nemesis-system mod for Red Dead Redemption 2. It makes the people enRAGE keeps track of act like they have their own lives, and it lets the whole world feel what you do. A few of them start to wonder whether any of it is real.

> **Unofficial and free.** This add-on isn't made by enRAGE's author or affiliated with them. It needs enRAGE installed and working, and never modifies enRAGE's program; the only enRAGE file it edits is enRAGE's settings file, when you change an enRAGE setting in RealNPCs' panel, after a backup. This repo contains none of enRAGE's code, content or files.
>
> **Story Mode only.** Don't run script mods in Red Dead Online.

**Version 0.6.2.** New in it: people's thoughts above their heads, conversations in their real recorded voices, trouble between people (a face into the bar, pickpockets, drunks), people flirting with and courting each other, Flirt in the game's lock-on menu, subtitles that look like the game's own, and enRAGE's own people joining in. Content notes: an adjustable chance (`AwakeningDespair`, default 75 percent, 0 turns it off) that a fully awake ordinary person ends their own life (with a gun that fits them, a knife, or by running at a wall); rough handling puts a man's face into the bar (`RoughHandling = false` turns it off); a man can be executed in front of you if you don't step in (`Executions = false` turns it off); the crying woman's knife and ambush are violent (`CryingWoman = false` turns her off); and a sick child's worry sometimes ends in a burial. If something misbehaves, please [open an issue](https://github.com/ninjaalex888/RealNPCs/issues) and attach your log.

## What it does

**People with lives.** Each town keeps its own people: up to 30 regulars per town, the same faces in their own clothes, still there on your next visit. Each ordinary person is living through something of their own (a debt, a sick child, a feud over a fence, a courtship) over about a week of game days, and when someone in town is on the other end of it, they think about it from their side too. The same people keep the same counters: every gunsmith, store keeper, barkeep and doctor is one person who remembers you, and when one dies a relative or a newcomer takes over. Over several meetings, the people enRAGE tracks tell you about themselves: their name, their work, their past, their family. They remember what you did, think about you, keep diaries, gossip, fall in love (adults only), marry, mourn, owe each other money, and come after you. Their personality (hothead, coward, proud, cold, greedy...) colours what they say.

**Minds.** People think about what is on their minds. Each keeps memories of what they lived through (a fight they were in or saw, a slight, a kindness, a theft, the drunk who called them out), and what they think is whatever pulls hardest right now: that, the worry they're living through, the weather, someone in sight, you. Memories fade, come back when someone in them walks by, and weigh differently on each temperament, so a coward and a hothead who saw the same fight think different things about it.

**The ticker.** What the people near you are privately thinking shows above their heads in small type, one person at a time (`ThoughtsOverHeads`; off, it goes on a thin line at the bottom of the screen). When nobody is near, that line shows a summary of the world and the latest news.

**Your deeds, and the rumours they start.** The add-on notices the towns and shops you visit, church and the saloon, hunting, killing, robbery, horse theft and cheating at cards. Self-defence isn't a crime: kill someone who came at you and nobody remembers it as a killing, and any bounty the fight gave you is put back. Witnesses remember; once one of them has lived to tell it, locals hear about it. Kill everyone who saw, or kill where nobody's looking, and nobody talks until the body is found, and then nobody knows who did it. Word travels from town to town, about one town a day, and grows in the telling. In Valentine it's "a man called Morgan shot a ranch hand". Further out it's "a devil on a black horse - they say bullets go right through him". Your deeds also turn up in enRAGE's newspapers.

**Letters, diaries and kin.** Letters wait for you at post offices, from the kin of people you killed, people you were good to, sweethearts, and people who suspect the truth. Loot a body and you may find the diary they kept. When you kill someone, their family far away hears of it. Some write; some ride west for revenge as one of enRAGE's own people.

**Life in the street.** People near you meet for reasons you can follow: a quarrel picks up again, sweethearts court, someone tries their luck with a person who caught their eye (and is answered by what that person makes of them; you see it in their thoughts on the ticker), a debt is called in, someone having a bad week snaps at whoever speaks to them. Bystanders turn to look and may take a side, and what happened is remembered: for days the two think about each other by name, and the town gossips about it. Strangers near you also catch cheats and propose. They square off in standoffs, sing old songs drunk, tell tall tales (some about you), read the paper aloud, haggle over horses, fall for a snake-oil pitch and pick pockets. You can step in: take a side, break up a fight (the town hears you're a peacemaker), or egg it on. Hold a gun on a stranger and they beg; lower it and word gets around that you showed mercy. Weddings happen at the church, and mourners speak at enRAGE's funerals.

**Flirting, and a night together.** Flirt with someone (F2, or Flirt in the game's lock-on menu) and they answer from what they think of you and who they are: their nature, faith, troubles and mood, against your money and your name in town. The saloon's women always go along, a single woman likely, a married one less so. Greet her first (R): it helps, and flirting cold hurts. When a woman says yes, Flirt becomes Hook up; her answer to that is its own, likelier after a greeting. Then the screen goes dark and an hour passes. With [CasualHookups](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/7608) installed, its own scene plays instead, and only with a woman who has said yes to you (see Install).

**Quiet when you stop.** Stand still for a few seconds, or watch through the cinematic camera, and enRAGE's tension stops building and no nemesis, gang or faction is sent after you. It uses enRAGE's own calm period, the one it uses after a fast travel, and picks up again soon after you move. Funerals and other planned events still go ahead.

**Feelings you can see.** Bars under the people near you show their fear of you, how much they like you and their anger at you. A small "Sore at you" panel in the top right lists the strangers whose grudge against you is building, with a mark where they'd become a nemesis. Either can be turned off.

**Encounters.** Away from the towns you may come upon one of a nearby town's own people on his knees at gunpoint, for a reason from his own life: a debt, a feud, a word to the law. Save him and the town hears of it; ride on and they shoot him. Out in the wilds late at night you may hear a woman crying somewhere off the road. Go close and she may come at you with a knife and run, or men may step out of the brush, or she may say something that makes no sense and walk off into the dark. Violent; one switch turns it off. And with West World on, people who woke and ran from their towns live rough in the wilds: come near their camp and they warn you off, or, if you keep your distance, tell you how it was. And after midnight, somewhere off in the dark, the awake stand in a silent ring: look away and they're closer.

**West World (one switch turns it all on or off).** Some people begin to suspect their world is made. At first it's deja vu and odd dreams; later they notice the loops, the same faces, "the music that comes before trouble". Now and then someone glitches. Some of the people you kill wake up days later and remember how they died and who did it. Now and then someone near you, often someone who knows you, starts following you at a distance and hides when you look. Stand still and they come closer and do odd things: kneel as at a graveside, lie down like the dead, tip a hat, hold up a hand for quiet, shake their head slowly, laugh at nothing.

**Voices.** Lines are spoken in the speaker's own recorded game voice: the recording that best fits the feeling of what they say, an unsettled one for the uncanny lines. What people say to you gets a subtitle while their voice plays (the recording's own words where they're known, else what they mean); talk you only overhear has none. Notices, gang and faction news, and enRAGE's territory changes are the game's own updates at the top left.

## Install

1. Install [enRAGE](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/10967) 0.9.5.3 and what it needs (Script Hook RDR2 and Script Hook RDR2 .NET V2), and make sure it works.
2. Download **[RealNPCs-0.6.2.zip](https://github.com/ninjaalex888/RealNPCs/releases/latest/download/RealNPCs-0.6.2.zip)** from the [latest release](https://github.com/ninjaalex888/RealNPCs/releases/latest) (it's also in the [release](release) folder). It holds `RealNPCs.dll` and a README.txt that explains every feature and setting.
3. Copy `RealNPCs.dll` into your game's `scripts` folder, next to enRAGE's `NemesisMod.dll`. To update, just replace the file: what people remember carries over, and your settings are kept. **Coming from enRAGE Westworld** (the old name): delete `EnrageWestworld.dll` from the scripts folder. RealNPCs won't run next to it, and copies your saves, diaries and settings over from the old folder the first time it starts.
4. Load your Story Mode save. About 10 seconds after you're in the world, the ticker appears at the bottom of the screen.
5. Optional: [CasualHookups](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/7608), a separate mod by its own author, plays an on-screen night together instead of RealNPCs' fade to black. Install it as its page says (`CasualHookups.asi` and `CasualHookups.ini` in the game folder). With it installed, its Flirt works only on a woman who has said yes to your flirting in RealNPCs (`CasualHookupsDecides`, on). The first time RealNPCs starts with it, it changes `CasualHookups.ini` once: its Flirt key moves from G (enRAGE's own lock-on prompts use G) to B, her clothes come back afterwards (enRAGE knows its people by their clothes), and it always gives in, since she has already said yes. The original file is kept as `RealNPCs\backup\CasualHookups.ini.before-realnpcs`. Restart the game once after that: CasualHookups may only read its settings when the game starts. Its settings are on the panel's Romance page. To keep the two mods apart, set `CasualHookupsDecides = false` and put the original file back.

To uninstall, delete `RealNPCs.dll`. You can also delete the `RealNPCs` folder in `Documents\Rockstar Games\Red Dead Redemption 2\` if you don't want to keep what people remembered.

## Keys

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Ctrl+F7 or Alt+F | The RealNPCs settings panel: RealNPCs, West World, Stalkers and every enRAGE setting in one place, with larger text. Press either again to close it. Alt+F11 opens it too, but only on enRAGE 0.9.5.3 |
| F7 | Shows what the nearest person enRAGE knows thinks of you, and writes a status line to the log. The town regulars and the fully awake around you show on the map for a minute |
| F2 | Flirt with the person you're looking at. Locked on to someone, the game's menu also offers Flirt on H |
| N | Read your letters, diaries and clippings, or take a diary you've just found |
| ] | Make a stalker now: someone near you (someone with a history with you first), or a stranger if nobody near can be. The log says what it's waiting for |
| [ | Stage an execution on the road near you, out of sight, to see one now (a mark on the map) |
| Shift+F7, Shift+F9, Shift+F2 | For testing: two people talk in their real voices; a face into the bar; the subtitle line in each font |
| (none) | Everything else RealNPCs can make happen near you now is a row on the settings panel's Testing page: Enter fires it, Right sets a key for it ([Testing] in the ini) |
| Y | Wake the person you are facing one stage (West World), so you can see what each stage does |
| T | The next line on the ticker now (on a long line, its next part first). T is enRAGE's telegraph key too: near a telegraph clerk, both happen |

Apart from T, none of these clash with enRAGE, Script Hook .NET's console (F8) or [Faster Looting](https://github.com/ninjaalex888/RDR2-FasterLooting). If another mod uses one, change it in the ini or the settings panel. Alt+F11 is enRAGE's own key for its settings. Only on enRAGE 0.9.5.3 does RealNPCs open its panel there in place of enRAGE's (`ReplaceEnrageSettings = false` gives it back); on other enRAGE versions Alt+F11 opens enRAGE's own panel, and Ctrl+F7 or Alt+F is the way in. About 15 seconds after loading, a notice says which key to use.

## Settings and log

Everything is kept in `Documents\Rockstar Games\Red Dead Redemption 2\RealNPCs\`:

- `RealNPCs.ini` holds the settings. Every feature can be turned off or tuned, each option has a comment, and saved changes take effect in game within a few seconds. The settings panel (Ctrl+F7 or Alt+F) edits it too, along with enRAGE's `NemesisMod.ini`, and CasualHookups' settings when that mod is installed.
- `RealNPCs.log` records what happened. Please attach it to bug reports.
- `backup\` holds a copy of `NemesisMod.ini` (and `CasualHookups.ini`) from before the panel first changed it in each session, and CasualHookups' original settings from before RealNPCs first set them.
- `arthur.json` and `john.json` hold what people know and feel about you. `diaries\` holds the diaries you've found, as text files.

The full guide to every feature and option is the [readme that comes with the mod](addon/EnrageWestworld/package/README.txt).

## Build from source

You need the [.NET SDK](https://dotnet.microsoft.com/download) (8 or newer) and two DLLs from your own install, which aren't included here:

- put `ScriptHookRDRNetAPI.dll` (from Script Hook RDR2 .NET V2) in `refs/`
- put `NemesisMod.dll` (from enRAGE, in the game's `scripts` folder) in `original/`

Then run:

```
cd addon/EnrageWestworld
dotnet build -c Release
```

The DLL is written to `addon/EnrageWestworld/bin/Release/net48/`. If the build says the .NET Framework 4.8 reference assemblies are missing, install the .NET Framework 4.8 Developer Pack. On Linux with Mono, add `-p:AutomaticallyUseReferenceAssemblyPackages=false -p:FrameworkPathOverride=/usr/lib/mono/4.8-api`.

The code is C# 7.3 for .NET Framework 4.8:

- `Core/` holds the parts that don't touch the game: people, rumours, letters, diaries, scenes and all the lines.
- `Game/` holds the parts that talk to the game and to enRAGE.

The bridge finds enRAGE's running script through Script Hook .NET and reads what enRAGE tracks. When RealNPCs adds to it (a memory, a thought, a deed, a news item, someone back from the dead or riding in for revenge, a marriage), it uses enRAGE's own methods wherever enRAGE has one, and otherwise sets the few things enRAGE has no method for and has enRAGE save them (the list is in CLAUDE.md). If a future enRAGE version changes something, only the feature that needs it switches off, and the log says which.

## Roadmap

The detailed plan, with the ideas, designs and build steps behind each item, is in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md).

1. **Conversations in their real voices.** People talk to each other with the game's own recorded lines, the ones it pairs for two people: one complains about the weather and another answers, one passes on a piece of gossip and another reacts, old friends greet each other. Every player hears them, with nothing extra to download. You hear overheard talk the way you'd overhear it in the street, with no subtitle, or, if you prefer, a short caption in the mod's own words. Anyone who builds a word list from their own game files on their own PC gets the exact words as subtitles. In: two people standing together near you remark on the weather, ask how the other is getting on, pass on a piece of news and react to it, or say hello; they remark on riders going by, tell a story about their town, and sweethearts pay each other compliments; and the game's crude remarks, in a saloon of an evening or between sweethearts at night (`CrudeTalk` leaves them out). Next: longer exchanges that run from a hello into news; friends ribbing each other, and the other answering back; someone pointing out a passer-by they have history with, warmly or not; people reacting aloud to what just happened in the street; and greetings in passing, part of the living street (17).
2. **Everyone is someone.** Every ordinary person in the world gets a name, a trade, a town and a temperament of their own, remembers what happens to them and can wake, not only the few that enRAGE tracks. In: residents, and the same people at the counters (46 shopkeepers, barkeeps, doctors and the like in 13 towns, who survive being shot or pass the post to a relative). Next: what they remember of you and deeds they witness, and each town's regulars, each with the same face and clothes, a routine (the store in the morning, the saloon at night, church on Sunday) and ties to each other, played by the people the game already puts in the street.
3. **The Awakening.** A few hosts slowly realise they're living in a loop. It shows more and more in what they think, say and do, until they break their script. In: the stages, what they do on screen at each, and the endings for the fully awake (pleading, running from town for good to live as a hermit, waking someone, kneeling, despair). Next, it spreads: awakened hosts talk about the loop where you can overhear them, try to wake others, and pass on a line that means something to them. They notice what can't be true, like a man they saw die walking around again. The ticker counts how many are stirring. The phases, in order: the loop, blind to what can't be true; reveries, gestures and dreams from other lives; counting the repeats; the voice, their routine heard as instructions and answered back; breaking the loop, refusing a routine and walking to the edge of town; the centre, when the voice turns out to be their own; and then either a reset back into the loop, which others notice, or staying awake and passing it on. The fully awake get more to do, too, besides pleading, fleeing, kneeling and despair. And despair with a gun or a knife gets an act of its own: today the person just stands there before it happens (the game has no animation for either, so it will be pieced together from ones it has); running at a wall already plays out. Loading an older save is felt too: someone killed in the lost hours gets a sharp déjà vu, and the waking talk of the morning that happened twice.
4. **Memory that behaves like memory.** Memories blur and get rewritten over time, unless they hurt. Places hold memories: ride past where you shot someone's brother and those who knew him react to the spot. People know you by your horse, your clothes and your habits, not only your face.
5. **Inner lives.** People hold opinions of each other, not just of you, which decides who gossips with whom and who takes whose side. They dream about what really happened to them. They change their minds, and their diaries show the turn. Thoughts come from each person's state: hunger at mealtimes, tiredness, cold and rain, money before payday, an old ailment, their mood, their temperament and the one worry they carry. They are written for the actual year in the game (1899, or 1907 in the epilogue). In: what's going on around them is on their minds too: a fight or a theft that just happened in front of them, the other people in sight (seen through their own nature: envy of the well-dressed, pity for the drunk, a wary eye on a hothead or on the man the whole town saw put a face into the bar), the rain down their collar, the saloon or the church they're standing in; and the small pleasures a life is mostly made of, between the worries.
6. **Interaction menu.** Locked on to someone you know, the game's own menu offers more than Greet: ask what's new or about someone they know, buy them a drink, give or lend money, apologise, warn them off, introduce two people, offer to help with their trouble, or flirt. What you do changes how they feel about you, and the town hears of it.
7. **Spreading saloon brawls.** One punch and half the saloon joins in. First, the trouble the game shows you, played out between other people, each time for a reason. In (not yet seen in game): a man with a grudge, or a hothead with drink in him, grabs another and smashes his face into whatever is at hand, a bar, a table, a piano or a wall, with the game's own move for it; a drunk in a saloon at night calls a man out, squares up, and falls on his face before a punch is thrown; the one it's done to reels, and anyone spoken to in a street scene takes it by their own nature (a hothead bristles, a coward flinches, the cold shake their heads). Fights come for reasons you can follow, and often enough to see: two hot-tempered men with bad blood come to blows at their first quarrel, and drink and temper in a saloon at night make a fistfight about one night in three. Next: the drunk really taking it outside; one punch drawing in the men around them; the barkeep and the law breaking it up.
8. **Answering letters.** Reply kindly, coldly, with a threat or with money, or ask to meet, and it changes what the writer does next: a grieving brother may stand down instead of riding west, or ride sooner. Later, write to anyone you've met.
9. **Narratives.** Every ordinary person's trouble can be changed: pay the debt, fetch the doctor, settle the feud, carry the courting note, or make it worse. Some grow into short stories over a few days (the son at the mine, the letter gone astray), and how it turns out changes their lives.
10. **Town events.** One framework, grown from the weddings: the Saturday dance, election day, the circuit judge and gallows day, the county fair, a Wild West show acting out your own legend, a barn raising. Each happens whether you're there or not, and you can step in.
11. **New arrivals.** Settlers, a mail-order bride, a preacher arrive by train, and people whose troubles end in leaving go the same way. Each town keeps a ledger: one where you've killed many thins out and grieves, one you've done right by grows.
12. **Natural voices.** An optional free offline voice engine (Piper), with a different voice for each person.
13. **Stalkers on horseback**, and revenants who turn up at camp.
14. **The game's real words.** Where the game's own recorded line is played, the subtitle shows what it actually says, and the add-on's own line moves to the ticker as the person's thought. Needs the game's speech audio turned into text once, offline.
15. **Inner voices from a local language model.** Full mode: a model running on the player's own graphics card (an RTX 5070 or better) writes people's thoughts, diaries and letters from their state and memories, ahead of time so the game never waits. It only words them: what people feel and do is still decided by the mod, and the written lines stay as the fallback.
16. **Night encounters.** Rare, unsettling things out in the wilds after dark, after the creepiest of the game's own (themes only, never its lines). In: the crying woman, the hermits who woke, the gathering. Next: a night visitor in a back street, crouched over a body, who is gone when you look away and turns up behind you after; an invitation to supper at a lonely homestead that ends with you waking in a ditch at dawn, robbed; and a voice calling for help from under a shop floor that nobody else seems to hear. Each with a version where it happens to someone else, and you hear about it. And from American and Cajun folklore, themes only: lights over the bayou, a bell ringing on a fresh grave, the knocking in Annesburg's mine.
17. **A living street.** Next after real voices. People treat each other the way the game treats you, using the game's own reactions aimed at each other and driven by what they know of each other. In this order: people who know each other acknowledge each other in passing (a tipped hat, a nod, a glare, a muttered word); the whole street reacts to a fight, a standoff or a death between other people, the way it does to yours; a known outlaw walking into town is stared at and stepped around, while the doctor or the preacher is greeted; people notice how someone looks (bloodied from a brawl, drunk, soaked) and comment, steer clear or help; and the town keeps time, its regulars at work by day, at the saloon in the evening, in church on Sunday morning and home at night (nobody walks across town: the regulars the game puts in the saloon at night are the ones whose routine takes them there). Already in, between people (not yet seen in game): pickpockets among the townsfolk, people short of money or light of finger who rob the well-dressed or the drunk, mostly unnoticed; honest people in the same trouble asking a stranger for a coin, and given one or sent off by the stranger's own nature; and anyone roughed up or robbed keeping clear of the one who did it, if they're the timid kind. The town remembers who did what.
18. **Moods that carry.** A bad morning makes for a short temper all day; good news softens a hard man for an afternoon; rain and cold wear people down. A mood lasts, colours what they think, say and do, and spreads from person to person in a crowd.
19. **Vices and habits.** The drinker drinks more after bad news and less on a Sunday; the gambler loses the rent and borrows; the pious keep the Sabbath and judge those who don't. Habits drive what people do as much as their worries.
20. **Grudges that travel.** Someone wronged tells their kin and their friends, and a brother takes it up: the trouble comes back around a week later, from someone the wrongdoer never met.
21. **Small kindnesses.** The counterweight to the trouble: people help someone up after a fall, carry a load, buy a round for a man with bad news, stand up for someone being bullied. Good turns are remembered too, and repaid. In: a friend goes to someone just roughed up or robbed, and the coin given to someone who asked.
22. **A mind underneath.** One model under everything people think, say and do: their traits, the life of their time and place, their needs through the day, the worry they carry, their feelings, moods, memories, ties and beliefs. What happens is judged by each person, felt, shaped by what 1899 lets them show, and shown through what the game can really do, while their thoughts say what their faces hide. RealNPCs keeps its own record of everyone, shared with enRAGE, so both mods build on the same person.
23. **The year as it was.** The real 1899 and 1907 calendars (Easter, the Fourth, Thanksgiving, Christmas, Mardi Gras in Saint Denis), the work of each season by region (calving, roundup, haying, the sugar harvest, trapping), and 1907's own news: the bank panic and the dry laws.
24. **Seeing what happened.** A who's who of the people you've met in the reader, an "Overheard" page of what went on near you, and a cue when voices are raised behind you.
25. **The epilogue remembers.** Arthur's world carries into John's, eight years older: married, widowed, moved on, and a few who remember a man called Morgan.
26. **Nights together.** In: women answer your flirting by chance (the saloon's always, single women likely, married ones less so, what they think of you tipping it), and a yes can lead to a night together, off screen. Next: where you go (a room upstairs, a hotel, her home), a price at the saloon, the morning after, sweethearts who expect you back, and a husband who finds out.
